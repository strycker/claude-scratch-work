# Cross-Phase UAT Audit — Phases 1 and 5

**Date:** 2026-09-09
**Trigger:** a percent-vs-decimal yield units defect (fixed 2026-09-08, commit `75dedc7`,
PR #140) revealed that both phases were signed off against output that was
arithmetically impossible.
**Scope:** Phases 1 and 5 only. Phases 2, 3, 4 not audited here.
**Verdict in one line:** neither phase failed *as written* — every criterion is
satisfied by the code that exists — but the recorded evidence values and the
diagnostic conclusions drawn from them are void, and the reason they were accepted
is a systemic gap in what the criteria ask for.

---

## 0. The mechanical audit reports "all clear" — that is the first finding

```
$ gsd-tools query audit-uat --raw
{"results": [], "summary": {"total_files": 0, "total_items": 0, ...}}
```

`audit-uat` scans for items marked `pending`, `skipped`, `blocked`, or
`human_needed`. Evidence that is **wrong but marked `passed`** is structurally
invisible to it. The tool cannot detect this class of problem, so its clean bill of
health is not evidence of anything. This audit was done by reading.

## 1. What was actually wrong

Two independent defects, both in Phase 1 code, both surviving Phase 1 sign-off, both
flowing into the Phase 5 evidence:

**D-A — yield units (fixed 2026-09-08).** FRED publishes `fred_gs10` in PERCENT
(4.68). `bond_price`/`monthly_total_return` are decimal-domain. `coupon_accrued =
yield_t0 / 12` therefore accrued a **39% monthly coupon**, and `long_duration_tr`
compounded to **2.3e128**. Separately the `cash` class returned a yield *level* that
every consumer then `pct_change()`d, so cash "returns" were month-over-month changes
in the yield — a T-bill going 0.5% → 3.0% booked **+500%**.

**D-B — ALFRED vintages (fixed 2026-08-05, quick task `260805-570`).**
`_REQUIRED_VINTAGE_ROLES` demanded a `realtime_end` column that `fredapi` has
commented out in its own source, so vintage ingestion failed **0/5 forever**. The
task's own plan states point-in-time vintages had "never once been fetched."

D-B matters here because it is **independent corroboration**: two unrelated defects,
same phase, same sign-off, same evidence shape. This is not one bad day.

## 3a. NEW DEFECT — found by applying the plausibility lens (open, unfixed)

I applied §4's proposed plausibility check to criterion 4 as a spot-test. It found a
live defect in under a minute, which is the strongest argument for §4 I can offer.

**`fred_cpi` contains two artificial ~3× discontinuities.** Directly observed in
`data/checkpoints/platform/monthly_raw.parquet`:

```
1970-12-31    39.60          1988-01-31   345.9
1971-01-31   119.03  (×3.0)  1988-02-29   115.9  (÷3.0)
```

Real CPIAUCSL on the 1982-84=100 base is ≈38 in 1970, ≈116 in 1988, ≈258 in 2020.
So the **pre-1971 and post-1988 segments are correct** and the **1971-01 → 1988-01
segment is inflated ~3×** — it is carrying a different index base (1967=100 or
similar).

**Mechanism.** `fred_cpi` (CPIAUCSL) is one of the five `fred_vintage` series.
Point-in-time reconstruction returns the value *as published at the time*, and CPI
vintages from the 1970s-80s were published on the **then-current index base**. Vintage
values are not level-comparable across rebasings. `align_agency_monthly` splices them
into one series as if they were.

**Consequence, verified:**
- `real_rate_level = fred_gs10 − CPI YoY×100` inherits it. Units are otherwise
  **correct** (median 2.14; 1981 Volcker +1.5…+4.3; 1974 inflation −3.0…−4.8 all
  economically right), but the range runs **−209.49 … +74.51**, with 24 impossible
  months clustered exactly in 1971 and 1988.
- `real_rate_level` is a **defining feature of states 2 and 3**, per the labeler's own
  diagnostics (`state 2: … high real_rate_level`, `state 3: low real_rate_level`).
  Those two states are **64.3% of total occupancy**.

**Scope.** In principle affects every rebased index-level vintage series: `fred_cpi`,
`fred_gdp` (GDPC1, chain-weighted, rebased), `fred_indpro` (rebased). `fred_unrate`
is a rate and `fred_payems` a headcount — both immune.

**Hypothesis, NOT yet verified:** this defect became *active* on 2026-08-05. Before
that, ALFRED returned 0/5 (defect D-B) so `align_with_fallback` used the shifted
modern-base series throughout, which is base-consistent. Fixing ALFRED is what let
mismatched-base vintages start flowing. If so, the corrupted CPI is five weeks old and
was not present in the Phase 1 or Phase 5 runs. **Worth confirming before assuming
either way.**

**Also not yet verified, but high-value:** whether the 1971 and 1988 breaks coincide
with detected regime transitions. Two artificial ±3× level shifts in a feature that
defines 64% of occupancy is a plausible contributor to the degenerate regime structure
(6 transitions in 59 years, 76-month detection lag). This should be checked before any
(K, λ) tuning is attempted — tuning against a corrupted feature would be wasted work,
and worse, would bake the corruption into the chosen hyperparameters.

**Priority: highest open item in this audit.** It is upstream of the labeler, and the
labeler's behaviour is the main open question in the project.

## 4. The systemic finding

Every criterion in both phases — including the ones I have marked genuinely verified
— is satisfied by evidence of one of four shapes:

1. **Existence** — "the function exists and is wired in"
2. **Shape** — "the artifact has the right columns and round-trips"
3. **Pure-function correctness** — "`bond_price(0.05, 0.05, 10)` prices to par"
4. **Placement** — "the ratio is the first metrics section"

**None of these four can detect a physically impossible value.** A pipeline emitting
`long_duration_tr = 2.3e128` satisfies all four. That is not a lapse in any one
sign-off; it is what the criteria asked for, and they were answered honestly.

Shape 3 deserves its own note because it is the most seductive. `bond_price` is
*correct*. Its test is *correct*. The test verifies the callee and says nothing about
whether the caller supplies the right units — and the units are the whole defect. A
green pure-function test next to a broken composed pipeline reads as reassurance.

The corollary at the tooling layer: `audit-uat` (§0) only surfaces items marked
pending/skipped/blocked. Neither the human gate nor the automated gate has a
mechanism that asks *is this number possible*.

### The missing criterion

Every phase that emits numeric output needs a **plausibility gate**: a stated
physically-possible range for each headline quantity, asserted in the automated suite
and restated in the verification evidence. Concretely, and cheap:

- research-series annualized return within, say, −20%…+30%, and annualized vol within
  0.1%…60%
- any total-return index bounded (e.g. terminal/initial < 10⁴ over 60 years)
- strategy mean monthly return |·| < 5%
- max drawdown within −100%…0%, and a "defensive" baseline never worse than SPY

Three such assertions now exist (`assert_yield_units_plausible`,
`TestKpisAreOnAPlausibleScale`, `test_percent_yields_produce_a_plausible_treasury_index`),
added with the units fix. They should be a *standing pattern*, not artifacts of one
incident.

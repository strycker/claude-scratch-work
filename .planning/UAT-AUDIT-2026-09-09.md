## 1. What was actually wrong

Two independent defects, both in Phase 1 code, both surviving Phase 1 sign-off, both
flowing into the Phase 5 evidence:

**D-A — yield units (fixed 2026-09-08).** FRED publishes `fred_gs10` in PERCENT
(4.68). `bond_price`/`monthly_total_return` are decimal-domain. `coupon_accrued =
yield_t0 / 12` therefore accrued a **39% monthly coupon**, and `long_duration_tr`
compounded to **2.3e128**. Separately the `cash` class returned a yield *level* that
every consumer then `pct_change()`d, so cash "returns" were month-over-month changes
in the yield — a T-bill going 0.5% → 3.0% booked **+500%**.

**D-B — ALFRED vintages (fixed 2026-08-05, quick task `260805-570`).**
`_REQUIRED_VINTAGE_ROLES` demanded a `realtime_end` column that `fredapi` has
commented out in its own source, so vintage ingestion failed **0/5 forever**. The
task's own plan states point-in-time vintages had "never once been fetched."

D-B matters here because it is **independent corroboration**: two unrelated defects,
same phase, same sign-off, same evidence shape. This is not one bad day.

## 3a. NEW DEFECT — found by applying the plausibility lens (FIXED 2026-09-09, `976f7c8`)

I applied §4's proposed plausibility check to criterion 4 as a spot-test. It found a
live defect in under a minute, which is the strongest argument for §4 I can offer.

**`fred_cpi` contains two artificial ~3× discontinuities.** Directly observed in
`data/checkpoints/platform/monthly_raw.parquet`:

```
1970-12-31    39.60          1988-01-31   345.9
1971-01-31   119.03  (×3.0)  1988-02-29   115.9  (÷3.0)
```

Real CPIAUCSL on the 1982-84=100 base is ≈38 in 1970, ≈116 in 1988, ≈258 in 2020.
So the **pre-1971 and post-1988 segments are correct** and the **1971-01 → 1988-01
segment is inflated ~3×** — it is carrying a different index base (1967=100 or
similar).

**Mechanism.** `fred_cpi` (CPIAUCSL) is one of the five `fred_vintage` series.
Point-in-time reconstruction returns the value *as published at the time*, and CPI
vintages from the 1970s-80s were published on the **then-current index base**. Vintage
values are not level-comparable across rebasings. `align_agency_monthly` splices them
into one series as if they were.

**Consequence, verified:**
- `real_rate_level = fred_gs10 − CPI YoY×100` inherits it. Units are otherwise
  **correct** (median 2.14; 1981 Volcker +1.5…+4.3; 1974 inflation −3.0…−4.8 all
  economically right), but the range runs **−209.49 … +74.51**, with 24 impossible
  months clustered exactly in 1971 and 1988.
- `real_rate_level` is a **defining feature of states 2 and 3**, per the labeler's own
  diagnostics (`state 2: … high real_rate_level`, `state 3: low real_rate_level`).
  Those two states are **64.3% of total occupancy**.

**Scope.** In principle affects every rebased index-level vintage series: `fred_cpi`,
`fred_gdp` (GDPC1, chain-weighted, rebased), `fred_indpro` (rebased). `fred_unrate`
is a rate and `fred_payems` a headcount — both immune.

**Hypothesis, NOT yet verified:** this defect became *active* on 2026-08-05. Before
that, ALFRED returned 0/5 (defect D-B) so `align_with_fallback` used the shifted
modern-base series throughout, which is base-consistent. Fixing ALFRED is what let
mismatched-base vintages start flowing. If so, the corrupted CPI is five weeks old and
was not present in the Phase 1 or Phase 5 runs. **Worth confirming before assuming
either way.**

**Also not yet verified, but high-value:** whether the 1971 and 1988 breaks coincide
with detected regime transitions. Two artificial ±3× level shifts in a feature that
defines 64% of occupancy is a plausible contributor to the degenerate regime structure
(6 transitions in 59 years, 76-month detection lag). This should be checked before any
(K, λ) tuning is attempted — tuning against a corrupted feature would be wasted work,
and worse, would bake the corruption into the chosen hyperparameters.

**FIXED 2026-09-09** (`976f7c8`, quick task `260909-0og`). `align_with_fallback` now
chains on growth measured *inside* each vintage — where the base cancels — and
ratio-splices the vintage segment onto `shift_series`' base at the handoff. With one
consistent base the ratios telescope, so the chained result equals the raw published
level exactly; rates and headcounts are provably untouched. A companion guard,
`_warn_on_level_discontinuity()`, flags >50% monthly steps on `kind: index` series and
was verified to flag exactly `1971-01-31` and `1988-02-29` on the real corrupted data.

**Still required:** a rebuild. This is an ingestion-side fix, so the committed
`monthly_raw.parquet` retains the corrupted `fred_cpi` until
`scripts/build_platform_data.py` is re-run with a live `FRED_API_KEY`.

**The two hypotheses in this section remain unverified** and are still worth checking
after the rebuild: whether the defect only became active on 2026-08-05 when ALFRED
started returning data, and whether the 1971/1988 breaks were driving the degenerate
regime structure. The second is the one that matters — it should be settled before any
(K, λ) tuning, since tuning against a corrupted feature would bake the corruption into
the chosen hyperparameters.

## 4. The systemic finding

Every criterion in both phases — including the ones I have marked genuinely verified
— is satisfied by evidence of one of four shapes:

1. **Existence** — "the function exists and is wired in"
2. **Shape** — "the artifact has the right columns and round-trips"
3. **Pure-function correctness** — "`bond_price(0.05, 0.05, 10)` prices to par"
4. **Placement** — "the ratio is the first metrics section"

**None of these four can detect a physically impossible value.** A pipeline emitting
`long_duration_tr = 2.3e128` satisfies all four. That is not a lapse in any one
sign-off; it is what the criteria asked for, and they were answered honestly.

Shape 3 deserves its own note because it is the most seductive. `bond_price` is
*correct*. Its test is *correct*. The test verifies the callee and says nothing about
whether the caller supplies the right units — and the units are the whole defect. A
green pure-function test next to a broken composed pipeline reads as reassurance.

The corollary at the tooling layer: `audit-uat` (§0) only surfaces items marked
pending/skipped/blocked. Neither the human gate nor the automated gate has a
mechanism that asks *is this number possible*.

### The missing criterion

Every phase that emits numeric output needs a **plausibility gate**: a stated
physically-possible range for each headline quantity, asserted in the automated suite
and restated in the verification evidence. Concretely, and cheap:

- research-series annualized return within, say, −20%…+30%, and annualized vol within
  0.1%…60%
- any total-return index bounded (e.g. terminal/initial < 10⁴ over 60 years)
- strategy mean monthly return |·| < 5%
- max drawdown within −100%…0%, and a "defensive" baseline never worse than SPY

Three such assertions now exist (`assert_yield_units_plausible`,
`TestKpisAreOnAPlausibleScale`, `test_percent_yields_produce_a_plausible_treasury_index`),
added with the units fix. They should be a *standing pattern*, not artifacts of one
incident.

move on every small change. The hysteresis stabilizes a **reported label**, not a
**portfolio**. That may well be the intended design — probability-weighted tilting
is smoother than hard switching, and it is defensible — but it is not what
criterion 3 says, and it means the act/unwind thresholds currently buy nothing in
turnover terms. **This needs an explicit decision: either wire hysteresis into
allocation, or reword the criterion to match the design.**

## What Part II adds to the systemic finding

§4's four evidence shapes hold up, and Part II adds two more specific failure
modes plus one strong positive:

6. **A criterion's purpose clause going untested.** Phase 4 C3 verified the
   mechanism named in the criterion and skipped the "so that…" that gave it a
   reason to exist. Purpose clauses are testable — this one predicts lower
   turnover — and should be.
7. **Structural proxy for a measured property.** Phase 3 C4: "uses a calibrator"
   standing in for "is calibrated". Whenever a criterion names a *quality*
   (calibrated, stable, accurate), the evidence needs a number and a stated band.
8. **The positive: oracle tests exist here and work.** Phase 3 C1 is the model.

**And the gap that spans all five phases: no criterion anywhere asks whether the
output is any good.** Phase 3 proved the DP decode is exactly optimal, and it is —
it is exactly decoding a degenerate solution of 6 transitions in 59 years with a
76-month detection lag. Phase 5 D-01 and Phase 6 D-16 make this deliberate
("passes on running and reporting correctly", "a negative verdict does not
block"), which is a reasonable tracer-bullet stance. But it means **the project
has no gate that can fail on a bad model, only on a broken one** — and that
should be a conscious choice re-examined at design freeze, not an accident.

vestigial **1.7%** and inflates state 2 to **half the sample**. Repaired, the five
states are far more balanced. The transition *count* is unchanged at 6, so the
break is not the cause of the long-sojourn degeneracy — but the *composition* is
badly distorted, and a 49.6/1.7 split cannot be dismissed as label permutation.

**Conclusion: fix the 1971 handoff before any (K, λ) tuning.** Tuning against a
labeling where one state holds 1.7% would select hyperparameters for a distorted

## N1 — NEW: the driver and the report label on different feature sets

Two different policies decide which features the labeler sees:

- `report._reference_label_columns` — keep only columns non-NaN across the whole
  decision range. Drops `fred_vix` (1990+) and `curve_10y2y` (1976+). **9 columns,
  695 months from 1963.**
- `driver._window_active_features` — admit any column with ≥ `feature_min_history`
  (120) months *in-window*. Admits `fred_vix`, and the any-NaN row drop then
  discards everything before **1990**.

So the smoothed reference labeling learns from 1963-2020 on 9 features, while the
walk-forward's per-step labeling learns from a different and often much shorter
window on a different feature set. The smoothed-vs-filtered gap is supposed to
isolate *label hindsight*; it currently also contains a **feature-set and
window difference**, which is a second uncontrolled variable in the project's
headline honesty metric — the same class of defect as the phantom-asset leak
fixed in `18f68af`.

This is a strong candidate explanation for the 61.5-month detection lag: the
filtered labeler is not trying to reproduce the same regimes the reference
defines.

## N2 — NEW: canonicalization silently falls back in early windows

The rebuild logs, on the first backtest step:

```
WARNING jump_model: trailing_return_1m not in feature_names —
        falling back to centroid column 0 for canonicalization sort order
```

`canonicalize_states` sorts states by `trailing_return_1m` precisely so labels
stay comparable across windows. When that column is not in the active set the
sort key changes, so **state 2 in one window need not mean state 2 in the next**.
Detection lag and label churn are both computed by comparing labels across
windows, so a silent change of sort key inflates both. Worth quantifying before
trusting either number.

# Part IV — A12 diagnosed and fixed, 2026-09-09

The live diagnostic settled it, and **the defect was not where the previous fix
put it.**

## What the live ALFRED frame showed

```
realtime_start range : 1972-07-21 -> 2026-08-12
earliest_vintage     : 1972-07-21

as_of 1970-12-31: known is EMPTY -> falls back to shift_series
as_of 1971-01-31: known is EMPTY -> falls back to shift_series
```

Both sides of the 1971 break are in the **fallback** branch — ALFRED's real-time
database for CPIAUCSL does not begin until 1972-07. So `align_with_fallback`'s
handoff ratio-splice was never reached, and could never have fixed this. The
2026-09-09 fix (`976f7c8`) was correct for the 1988 rebasing and irrelevant here.

## The actual cause

`_shift_fallback_series` picked each reference period's **first-published**
value. ALFRED's first vintage (1972-07-21) covers only reference periods from
**1970-12** onward, so the pick crosses a base boundary at exactly that point:

| reference period | earliest available row | base | value |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1970-11 | 1994-02-17 vintage | 1982-84=100 | 39.6 |
| **1970-12** | **1972-07-21 vintage** | **1967=100** | **119.03** |

After `.shift(1)` that lands on 1971-01 — the observed break, to the month.

## Fix

Read one consistent vintage: the **latest** revision per reference period, not
the first-published one. Base-consistent by construction, and it is what the
docstring's own cited convention (the incumbent's publication-lag `shift()`,
ADR #7) actually does — the incumbent shifts the *current* series.

Nothing point-in-time is lost. `align_with_fallback` consults this series only
for as-of dates *before* the earliest vintage, where no vintage existed, so
"first published" was already a later revision there anyway. Also hardened
against fredapi's `NaT`-valued rows, which appear in the real frame (e.g.
`1972-08-22 | 1970-12-01 | NaT`) and must not win the per-period pick.

2 new tests, both failing pre-fix, built from the real frame's shape.

## Bonus finding — `.env` is only read as a side effect

`load_dotenv()` is called *inside* `load_platform_config()`
(`platform/config.py:77`), so importing the library does not read `.env`. Any
script that checks `os.environ["FRED_API_KEY"]` before calling
`load_platform_config()` will wrongly report the key as missing — which is
exactly what `diagnose_cpi_handoff.py` did on a machine whose `.env` had it all
along. `scripts/build_platform_data.py` avoids this by calling `load_dotenv()`
explicitly (line 73). Worth knowing when writing any new script.

## A13 — CONFIRMED, and larger than first described. Now the top open item.

The walk-forward's active feature set is not merely *different* from the
reference's — it **changes 7 times** during the backtest:

| from | n_active |
|---|---|
| 1972-01 | 4 |
| 1972-02 | 6 |
| 1972-04 | 8 |
| 1973-02 | 9 |
| 1986-06 | 10 |
| 1995-02 | 12 |

purely detection delay; it also contains a feature-set difference that grows and
shrinks over time.
